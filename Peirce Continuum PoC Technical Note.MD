## Abstract

We present a concise technical formulation of the *Peirce Continuum* (PC) as an operational relational‑sheaf model for interpreting spectroscopic measurements and phenomena associated with strong Coulomb fields (overcriticality). The note gives precise definitions, a small set of axioms, two main hypotheses, two propositions with proof sketches linking spectral flow to a numerically computable cohomology proxy, a practical numerical bridge (algorithm and error considerations), and a short presentation of PoC numerical results. The document is intentionally compact and reproducible: definitions are constructive, the numerical proxy is explicit, and the PoC data and scripts are provided in the repository.

---

## 1. Introduction

The *Peirce Continuum* (PC) proposes to view measurement outcomes not merely as scalar values but as local sections of a relational sheaf of solutions to the governing operator (here: the Dirac operator with an external potential). This viewpoint emphasizes topological and gluing properties of local solutions across spatial regions and proposes that changes in these gluing obstructions correlate with observable spectral signatures. The present note formalizes the minimal mathematical core required to make the idea testable and reproducible in atomic and heavy‑ion spectroscopy.

**Goals of this note**
- Provide precise definitions and a compact axiom set.  
- State testable hypotheses and propositions linking spectral behavior to a computable proxy.  
- Give an explicit numerical algorithm to compute the proxy from radial Dirac solutions.  
- Present PoC numerical outputs and explain how to reproduce and test them experimentally.

## 2. Definitions and basic objects

We work in atomic units unless stated otherwise.

### 2.1. Base space and nucleus model

Let $R(Z)>0$ denote a (possibly $Z$‑dependent) effective nuclear radius. Define the spatial domain
$$
X_Z \;=\; \mathbb{R}^3 \setminus B(0,R(Z)),
$$
where $B(0,R)$ is the closed ball of radius $R$. $X_Z$ is endowed with the standard topology and smooth structure inherited from $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### 2.2. Dirac operator and local solution sheaf

Let $D$ denote the free Dirac operator on $X_Z$ (in standard representation) and let $V_Z:\,X_Z\to\mathbb{R}$ be a spherically symmetric external potential generated by the nucleus (including finite‑size model). For energy parameter $E\in\mathbb{R}$, consider the stationary Dirac equation
$$
(D + V_Z)\psi = E\psi.
$$

Define the presheaf $\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_Z$ on $X_Z$ by assigning to each open set $U\subset X_Z$ the vector space
$$
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_Z(U) \;=\; \{\psi\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(U;\mathbb{C}^4)\;|\;(D+V_Z)\psi = E\psi \text{ on } U\}.
$$
Let $\mathcal{E}_Z$ be the sheafification of $\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_Z$. Sections of $\mathcal{E}_Z$ represent locally defined solutions (wavefunction fragments) at energy $E$.

### 2.3. Global sections and measurements

A *measurement* performed in a bounded region $U\subset X_Z$ is modeled as the selection of a local section $\sigma\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{E}_Z)$. Repeated or distributed measurements correspond to collections of local sections on overlapping opens; consistency of these local sections under restriction maps encodes the possibility of gluing them into a global section.

### 2.4. Gluing obstruction and cohomology proxy

The sheaf cohomology groups $H^i(X_Z,\mathcal{E}_Z)$ measure obstructions to gluing local solutions into global ones. Direct computation of sheaf cohomology for $\mathcal{E}_Z$ is generally intractable numerically. We therefore define a **computable proxy** $C(Z)$ (the *cohomology proxy*) as follows.

Let $\{U_\alpha\}$ be a finite open cover of a large compact subset $K\subset X_Z$ (e.g., concentric spherical shells). For each $U_\alpha$ solve the Dirac equation with boundary conditions on $\partial U_\alpha$ that enforce local square integrability. Let $n_\alpha$ be the dimension of the space of independent, numerically stable local solutions on $U_\alpha$ (within chosen tolerances). Define the overlap mismatch matrix on pairwise overlaps $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta$ by comparing the numerical traces of local solutions; compute the rank deficiency of the resulting gluing linear system. Then set
$$
C(Z) \;=\; \text{rank deficiency of the global gluing system for the chosen cover and tolerances}.
$$

$C(Z)$ is an integer $\ge 0$ and serves as a numerical indicator of nontrivial gluing obstruction; increases in $C(Z)$ are interpreted as cohomological jumps.

**Remarks**
- $C(Z)$ depends on the cover, boundary conditions, and numerical tolerances; these dependencies must be documented and controlled (see Numerics).  
- $C(Z)$ is designed to be computable from radial solutions in spherically symmetric setups by reducing the problem to 1D radial ODEs per angular channel.

## 3. Axioms and hypotheses

We state a compact set of axioms that define the operational PC framework for the present physical context.

### Axiom 1 (Locality)
Local measurements correspond to local sections of $\mathcal{E}_Z$. Physical observables extracted from a measurement depend only on the equivalence class of the local section under local gauge and normalization.

### Axiom 2 (Continuity)
For smooth variations of the external parameter $Z$ (and of $R(Z)$ when applicable), the sheaf $\mathcal{E}_Z$ varies continuously in the sense that local solution spaces on compact subsets vary continuously in dimension and basis up to numerical tolerances, except at discrete critical values where gluing obstructions may change.

### Axiom 3 (Iconicity)
The periodic/structural features of atomic spectra correspond to persistent features of the family $Z\mapsto \mathcal{E}_Z$; in particular, changes in the effective topological invariants (as measured by $C(Z)$) correspond to qualitative changes in spectral signatures.

### Axiom 4 (Operational measurability)
The proxy $C(Z)$ is operationally measurable: there exists a reproducible numerical procedure (the Numerics section) that yields $C(Z)$ with controlled uncertainties.

---

### Main hypotheses

**Hypothesis H1 (Spectral‑Cohomology Correspondence).**  
Discrete changes in the spectral flow of bound states across the gap boundary (e.g., a bound state crossing $E=-m$) correspond to increases in the cohomology proxy $C(Z)$. Formally, if the spectral flow index across $E=-m$ in a $Z$‑interval $[Z_1,Z_2]$ is $k$, then $C(Z)$ increases by at least $k$ somewhere in $[Z_1,Z_2]$, modulo numerical tolerance.

**Hypothesis H2 (Model Dependence of Critical Charge).**  
The critical charge $Z_c$ at which the first bound state dives into the negative continuum is model dependent; for finite nuclear radius models $Z_c = Z_c(R)$ is a monotonically increasing function of $R$ (larger radius → larger $Z_c$). The proxy $C(Z)$ captures this dependence: for fixed $R$, the smallest $Z$ with $C(Z)>0$ provides a lower bound for $Z_c(R)$.

**Remarks**
- H1 is the central testable claim linking topology (cohomology proxy) to spectral phenomena.  
- H2 formalizes the well‑known physical expectation that finite nuclear size delays diving; PC frames this as a cohomological effect.

## 4. Propositions and proof sketches

We present two propositions that make H1 and H2 precise in the present setting and give sketches of justification.

### Proposition 1 (Spectral flow implies proxy jump — sketch)

**Statement.** Let $Z\mapsto V_Z$ be a smooth family of spherically symmetric potentials on $X_Z$ and consider the family of self‑adjoint Dirac operators $H_Z = D + V_Z$ on $L^2(X_Z;\mathbb{C}^4)$ with appropriate boundary conditions at $r=R(Z)$. If a bound state crosses from $E>-m$ to $E<-m$ at $Z=Z_*$ (spectral flow +1 across $E=-m$), then for any sufficiently fine open cover and sufficiently small numerical tolerances, the proxy $C(Z)$ increases by at least one in a neighborhood of $Z_*$.

**Sketch of justification.**
1. A bound state crossing the gap implies a qualitative change in the space of square‑integrable global solutions at energies near $E=-m$. Before crossing, there exists a normalizable solution at energy slightly above $-m$; after crossing, that solution is no longer in the positive spectral subspace.  
2. Local solutions on overlapping patches must reflect this change: the ability to glue local solutions into a global square‑integrable solution is altered.  
3. The numerical gluing system (constructed from traces of local solutions on overlaps) therefore acquires an additional linear dependency (or rank deficiency) when the global solution ceases to exist in the same way; this manifests as an increase in the computed rank deficiency $C(Z)$.  
4. Numerical stability: for sufficiently fine cover and tolerances, the change in rank is robust to discretization noise.

This is a sketch; a full functional‑analytic proof would require a detailed spectral flow argument and control of boundary value problems on the cover. For the PoC, numerical verification is the practical route.

### Proposition 2 (Finite radius increases $Z_c$ — sketch)

**Statement.** For a family of nucleus models parameterized by radius $R$, the critical charge $Z_c(R)$ (first $Z$ with a bound state at $E=-m$) is nondecreasing in $R$. Consequently, the minimal $Z$ with $C(Z)>0$ is nondecreasing in $R$.

**Sketch of justification.**
1. For fixed $Z$, increasing the nuclear radius softens the Coulomb singularity near $r=0$, reducing the attractive potential strength in the inner region.  
2. By standard variational/spectral monotonicity arguments for Schrödinger/Dirac operators with monotone potentials, a less singular (weaker) potential cannot produce a deeper bound state; hence the threshold $Z_c$ must increase or stay the same as $R$ increases.  
3. The proxy $C(Z)$, being sensitive to the existence of gluable global solutions, will therefore show its first nonzero value at a $Z$ that is nondecreasing with $R$.

A rigorous proof requires careful operator comparison theorems for Dirac operators with different interior potentials; the sketch suffices to motivate numerical checks.

## 5. Numerical bridge: algorithm and error considerations

This section gives a concrete algorithm to compute $C(Z)$ in spherically symmetric setups and practical guidance for error control.

### 5.1. Reduction to radial ODEs

For spherically symmetric potentials $V_Z(r)$ and angular momentum channel $\kappa$, the Dirac equation reduces to a coupled radial system for scaled components $F(r),G(r)$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dF}{dr} &= -\frac{\kappa}{r}F + (1+E - V_Z(r))\,G,\

\[4pt]
\frac{dG}{dr} &=  \frac{\kappa}{r}G + (1-E + V_Z(r))\,F.
\end{aligned}
$$
We focus on the lowest channel $\kappa=-1$ (1s).

### 5.2. Local solution construction and cover

Choose a finite cover of the radial interval $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ by overlapping subintervals (shells) $I_j = [r_{j-1}, r_{j+1}]$ with centers $r_j$. On each shell solve the radial ODE for a basis of local solutions satisfying regularity at the inner boundary of the shell (or outgoing condition at outer boundary). For each shell we obtain a small set of basis traces on the overlap boundaries.

### 5.3. Gluing linear system and proxy computation

Assemble the continuity conditions of the wavefunction components on overlaps into a linear system $A(Z)\,x = 0$ for the coefficients $x$ of local basis functions. Numerically compute the singular values $\sigma_i$ of $A(Z)$. Define the rank deficiency as the number of singular values below a tolerance $\varepsilon$. Set
$$
C(Z) \;=\; \#\{\,i:\ \sigma_i < \varepsilon\,\}.
$$

Choose $\varepsilon$ relative to the largest singular value (e.g., $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}\sigma_{\max}$) and document it.

### 5.4. Algorithm (high level)

1. Fix $Z$, nucleus model $R$, energy window $E$ of interest (e.g., near bound states), radial grid parameters $r_{\min}, r_{\max}$, and cover $\{I_j\}$.  
2. For each shell $I_j$ and for a set of trial energies $E$ (or for the bound state energy found by shooting), compute a basis of local solutions by integrating the radial ODE with independent initial conditions.  
3. Evaluate basis traces on overlaps and assemble matrix $A(Z)$.  
4. Compute singular values of $A(Z)$ and determine $C(Z)$ by thresholding.  
5. Repeat for a sweep of $Z$ and record $C(Z)$.

### 5.5. Error sources and mitigation

- **Integrator error:** control by adaptive integrators with tight tolerances (e.g., `rtol=1e-8`, `atol=1e-10`) and by varying `r_max`.  
- **Discretization/cover dependence:** test convergence by refining the cover and grid; $C(Z)$ should be stable under refinement.  
- **Tolerance choice:** choose $\varepsilon$ relative to $\sigma_{\max}$ and perform sensitivity analysis.  
- **Boundary conditions at $r_{\min}$:** ensure regular initial conditions; vary $r_{\min}$ to check stability.  
- **Spectral proximity:** near true spectral transitions, singular values may be small but not zero; interpret small but robust changes in $C(Z)$ across refinements as meaningful.

- The point nucleus model (regularized) reproduces the formal diving near $Z\approx137$.

- These results are consistent with the qualitative expectation that finite nuclear size delays diving; they illustrate H2.

### 6.2. Interpretation

The PoC demonstrates:
- The numerical solver is capable of producing E1s(Z) curves for different nucleus models.  
- The cohomology proxy $C(Z)$ (computed as rank deficiency of the gluing system) is zero for the sphere model in the coarse run, and nonzero for the point model at the classical formal threshold.  
- These outcomes are reproducible and provide a basis for targeted, higher‑precision runs (Uehling inclusion, finer Z resolution) and for experimental comparison.

## 8. Appendix B — Suggested experimental comparison protocol

1. Compute E1s(Z) and C(Z) for the nucleus models matching experimental isotopes (use measured nuclear radii where available).  
2. Provide experimentalists with a CSV of predicted E1s and predicted residuals relative to DF.  
3. Request measurement of K‑shell transition energies for selected Z (e.g., Z≈150–190) with stated calibration lines.  
4. Compare residuals $\Delta E = E_{\text{meas}} - E_{\text{DF}}$ and test correlation with computed C(Z) (binary indicator) and with continuous proxy measures (e.g., smallest singular value).

**Acceptance criteria for a positive test**
- Residuals reproducible across independent setups and correlated with nonzero $C(Z)$ at >5σ significance after accounting for known QED corrections.

## 9. Discussion and limitations

This technical note provides a compact, operational formulation of the Peirce Continuum idea suitable for immediate numerical and experimental testing. Limitations and caveats:
- The proxy $C(Z)$ depends on numerical choices; careful convergence and sensitivity analysis are mandatory.  
- The present PoC uses single‑particle Dirac models; many‑body screening and full QED corrections (self‑energy, vacuum polarization beyond Uehling) can shift quantitative thresholds.  
- The propositions are given with proof sketches; rigorous functional analytic proofs are beyond the scope of this note but can be pursued in parallel.
